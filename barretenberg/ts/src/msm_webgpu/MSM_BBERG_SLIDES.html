The MSM problem

Compute the multi-scalar multiplication:

$$ S \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} [s_i]\, P_i $$

  • $P_i \in \mathbb{G}_1(\text{bn254})$  —  affine elliptic points, $n$ up to $2^{20}$
  • $s_i \in \mathbb{F}_r$  —  scalars, each $\lambda = 254$ bits
  • $S \in \mathbb{G}_1$  —  single output point

For a Honk-style prover: 20–50 MSMs per proof against a fixed SRS. The wall-clock of MSM is the wall-clock of the prover.

Why Pippenger?

Naive (scalar-mul each, then sum): $$ \text{cost}_{\text{naive}} \;\approx\; n \cdot \lambda \;=\; 2^{20} \cdot 254 \;\approx\; 2.7 \times 10^8\ \mathbb{G}_1\text{-ops} $$
Pippenger (window size $c$, $T = \lceil \lambda/c \rceil$ windows): $$ \text{cost}_{\text{pip}} \;\approx\; \underbrace{n \cdot T}_{\text{bucket fills}} \;+\; \underbrace{2 \cdot 2^c \cdot T}_{\text{bucket reduce}} $$

At $n = 2^{20}$, $c = 14$: $\approx 2 \times 10^7$ ops  ⇒  $\sim 14\times$ speedup.

Window decomposition

Slice each scalar into $T$ chunks of $c$ bits:

$$ s_i \;=\; \sum_{j=0}^{T-1} s_{i,j}\,2^{jc}, \qquad s_{i,j} \in [0, 2^c) $$

Substitute back into the MSM:

$$ S \;=\; \sum_{j=0}^{T-1} 2^{jc}\, W_j, \qquad W_j \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} [s_{i,j}]\, P_i $$

Each $W_j$ is a sub-MSM with $c$-bit scalars instead of $\lambda$-bit.

For bn254 at $c = 15$: $T = 17$ windows.

Bucketing within one window

Within window $j$, group inputs by their digit value:

$$ B_{j,k} \;=\; \sum_{\,i\,:\, s_{i,j} = k\,} P_i, \qquad k \in [1, 2^c) $$

Then the window sum is a small weighted-sum of buckets:

$$ W_j \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{2^c - 1} k \cdot B_{j,k} $$

Cost per window: $\approx n$ adds (fill buckets) + $\approx 2 \cdot 2^c$ adds (reduce).

Suffix-sum reduction

Naively $W_j = 1 \cdot B_1 + 2 \cdot B_2 + \cdots + (B{-}1) \cdot B_{B{-}1}$ — needs scalar muls.

Rewrite as a double sum:

$$ W_j \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{B-1}\;\sum_{k=t}^{B-1} B_{j,k} $$

$\Rightarrow$ two-accumulator walk:

m ← B[B-1];   g ← m
for k = B-2 down to 1:
    m ← m + B[k]      // running sum
    g ← g + m         // running-of-running
W_j ← g

Cost: $2(B{-}1)$ G$_1$ additions per window. No scalar muls.

Each bucket $B_k$ ends up multiplied by its index $k$ — the count of how many suffixes include it.

Signed digits halve the bucket count

If a digit exceeds $2^{c-1}$, rewrite with a borrow:

$$ s_{i,j} \;=\; -(2^c - s_{i,j}) \;+\; 1 \cdot 2^c $$

Propagate the $+1$ as a carry into $s_{i,j+1}$. Result:

$$ s_{i,j} \in \big[-2^{c-1},\; 2^{c-1}\big),\qquad B = 2^{c-1} $$

Negative digit ⇒ negate the point. In affine coordinates:

$$ -(x, y) \;=\; (x, -y). $$

Free: just flip $y$. Bucket array halves.

Top-level pipeline

Inputs
$n$ scalars $\{s_i\}_{i=0}^{n-1} \subset \mathbb{F}_r$ and $n$ base points $\{P_i\} \subset \mathbb{G}_1$.
↓
1 · Compute per-scalar weights
$w_i \;=\; \lceil \lambda_i / c \rceil \,+\, 4$   (FIXED_PER_SCALAR_WEIGHT = 4)
↓
2 · Partition by weight → $K$ work units
Greedy split into $K$ contiguous index ranges with $\sum_{i \in \text{unit}_k} w_i \approx \tfrac{1}{K}\sum_i w_i$.
↓
3 · parallel_for over $K$ pthreads
thread 0
FULL Pippenger on unit$_0$  ⇒  $S_0$
thread 1
FULL Pippenger on unit$_1$  ⇒  $S_1$
… thread $K{-}1$
FULL Pippenger on unit$_{K-1}$  ⇒  $S_{K-1}$
Each thread runs all $T$ windows on a private bucket array of size $2^{c-1} \times 64$ B.
↓
4 · Reduce across threads
$S \;=\; \displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} S_k$   — $K{-}1$ G$_1$ adds (negligible).

Key choice: parallelise across inputs, not across windows. No atomic contention on the bucket array.

Per-thread inner loop

For window $j = T{-}1$ down to $0$  (MSB-first $\Rightarrow$ Horner):

1 Build schedule  —  for each $i$ in the work unit, compute $k = \text{signed\_slice}_j(s_i)$. If $k \neq 0$, push entry $(i, k)$.
2 Radix-sort schedule  —  MSD, 8-bit, depth $\leq 4$, in-place. Groups by bucket; counts zero-digits as side-effect.
3 Accumulate into buckets  —  batched-affine + recirculation (hot loop). Output: $\{B_{j,k}\}_{k=1}^{2^{c-1}-1}$.
4 Reduce buckets to $W_j$  —  suffix-sum walk: $W_j = \sum_k k \cdot B_{j,k}$ in $2(B{-}1)$ G$_1$ adds.
5 Horner combine  —  $S \leftarrow S \cdot 2^c + W_j$   (interleaved with the window loop).

Schedule entry layout

Each (point index, bucket index) packed into one uint64_t:

$$ \text{entry} \;=\; \big(\text{point\_idx} \,\ll\, 32\big) \;\;|\;\; \text{bucket\_idx} $$

point_idx bits 63 ↑ 32  ·  32 bits
bucket_idx bits 31 ↓ 0  ·  low $c$ bits used

Sort by the low bits $\Rightarrow$ identical bucket values become adjacent in memory.

The 32-bit point_idx caps MSM at $2^{32}$ inputs — irrelevant for any realistic SRS.

MSD radix sort by bucket

Algorithm
$\texttt{RADIX\_BITS} = 8$  ·  one byte per pass, in-place, no aux buffer.
Recursion depth $\leq 4$ (the 32-bit bucket field, 8 bits per pass).
Free side-effect
Count of entries with bucket = 0 (zero-digit scalars) is harvested in the first pass  ⇒  downstream skips them.
Why MSD
Cache-friendly: each pass touches one byte of each entry, sequential reads + sequential bucket writes within the recursion.
Output shape
Schedule array grouped by bucket. Consecutive entries either share a bucket or transition to the next one — exactly what the 4-case classifier expects.

The 4-case classifier — process_bucket_pair

Take two consecutive schedule entries $\text{lhs},\ \text{rhs}$. Compute:

$$ \text{same} \;=\; \big(\text{lhs.bucket} \,=\, \text{rhs.bucket}\big),\qquad \text{has\_acc} \;=\; \text{active}[\text{lhs.bucket}] $$

samehas_accaction
TTqueue $(\,\text{acc}[b],\ \text{rhs}\,)$;  acc kept
TFqueue $(\,\text{lhs},\ \text{rhs}\,)$;  no acc set
FTqueue $(\,\text{acc}[b],\ \text{lhs}\,)$;  acc cleared
FFcache $\text{lhs} \rightarrow \text{acc}[b]$;  nothing queued

Implementation: no branches. Boolean × pointer math + a sentinel null_location so unconditional stores have a safe target.

Affine addition: the math

For curves with $a = 0$ (like bn254), adding $P = (x_1, y_1)$, $Q = (x_2, y_2)$:

$$ \mu \;=\; \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} $$

$$ x_3 \;=\; \mu^2 - x_1 - x_2 $$

$$ y_3 \;=\; \mu(x_1 - x_3) - y_1 $$

Cost per add: $1\ \mathbb{F}_q\text{-inv} \,+\, 2\ \mathbb{F}_q\text{-mul} \,+\, 1\ \mathbb{F}_q\text{-sqr} \,+\, 6\ \mathbb{F}_q\text{-sub}$.

That single inversion is brutal: $|I| \approx 332 |M|$ in Barretenberg's cost model.

Montgomery batch inversion

Given $m$ independent denominators $\delta_1, \ldots, \delta_m$, compute all $m$ inverses sharing one inversion.

Build prefix products:

$$ \pi_k \;=\; \delta_1 \cdot \delta_2 \cdots \delta_k, \quad k = 1, \ldots, m $$

One inversion of the top:

$$ \rho_m \;=\; \pi_m^{-1} $$

Back-substitute to extract each $\delta_k^{-1}$:

$$ \delta_k^{-1} \;=\; \rho_k \cdot \pi_{k-1}, \qquad \rho_{k-1} = \rho_k \cdot \delta_k $$

Step-by-step on $m = 3$

Given $\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3$.

stepcomputestate
prefix$\pi_1 = \delta_1$1 mul
prefix$\pi_2 = \pi_1 \cdot \delta_2$1 mul
prefix$\pi_3 = \pi_2 \cdot \delta_3$1 mul
invert$\rho_3 = \pi_3^{-1}$1 inv
extract$\delta_3^{-1} = \rho_3 \cdot \pi_2;\quad \rho_2 = \rho_3 \cdot \delta_3$2 muls
extract$\delta_2^{-1} = \rho_2 \cdot \pi_1;\quad \rho_1 = \rho_2 \cdot \delta_2$2 muls
extract$\delta_1^{-1} = \rho_1$0 muls

Total: $3m - 3$ muls + 1 inv = $\approx 3m\ |M| + |I|$ for $m$ inverses.

Amortised cost per affine add

Per pair: 1 sub + (1 mul for delta) + (1 from batch-invert overhead $3|M|$) + (1 mul for $\mu$) + (1 sqr + further muls for $x_3, y_3$):

$$ \boxed{\ \text{cost}_{\text{add}} \;\approx\; 3 |M| \;+\; \frac{|I|}{m}\ } $$

With $m = 1024$ and $|I| \approx 332 |M|$: per-add $\approx 3.3 |M|$.

For comparison, Jacobian mixed-add is $\approx 11 |M|$.

$\Rightarrow$ $\sim 3.3\times$ speedup on the hottest path.

Why recirculate outputs?

Without recirculation:
Outputs of batch-add ⇒ random-access writes to bucket array.
Bucket array is $2^{c-1} \times 64$ B = $\sim 1$ MB at $c=15$.
Every write is a guaranteed L1 cache miss.
With recirculation:
Outputs feed back into the classifier.
Two consecutive outputs sharing a bucket get queued again instead of written.
The radix sort already groups by bucket → many adjacent same-bucket outputs.
Bucket-array writes are minimised; most adds flow through the scratch buffer.

Why 9 × 29 on WASM?

WASM has no __int128, no efficient $64 \times 64 \to 128$ mul.

Bn254 base-field element ($\log_2 q \approx 254$) needs a custom limb representation.

$$ a \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} a_i\, 2^{wi},\qquad a_i \in [0, 2^w) $$

Constraints:

$$ L \cdot w \;\geq\; 254 \qquad \text{(fits the field)} $$

$$ 2w + \lceil\log_2 L\rceil \;\leq\; 64 \qquad \text{(partial-product accumulator)} $$

Integer solution: $w \in \{29, 30\}$. Pick $w = 29$ for headroom.

Window 0 — suffix-sum reduce: the trick

We need $W_0 = \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{B-1} k \cdot B_k$ but want to avoid scalar multiplications.

Replace $k \cdot B_k$ with a sum:

$$ k \cdot B_k \;=\; \underbrace{B_k + B_k + \cdots + B_k}_{k \text{ copies}} \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{k} B_k $$

Now swap the order of summation:

$$ W \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{B-1} \sum_{t=1}^{k} B_k \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{B-1}\, \underbrace{\sum_{k=t}^{B-1} B_k}_{m_t}$$

Define $m_t$ as the suffix sum from $t$. Then:

$$\boxed{\;W \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{B-1} m_t \;=:\; g\;}$$

Algorithm — two running accumulators

From the rows we extract the recurrence:

$$ m_{B-1} = B_{B-1}, \qquad m_{t-1} = m_t + B_{t-1}. $$

Walk $t$ from $B{-}1$ down to $1$, maintaining $m$ (current suffix sum) and $g$ (running sum of $m$'s):

m ← B[B-1];   g ← m
for k = B-2 down to 1:
    m ← m + B[k]      # m_{t-1} = m_t + B_{t-1}
    g ← g + m         # accumulate g
W_j ← g

Cost: $2(B{-}1)$ G$_1$ additions per window. No scalar muls.

Note: empty bucket leaves $m$ unchanged, but $g \mathrel{+}{=} m$ still fires — this is correct because $m_t$ still "spans" the empty $B_t$ slot.

Horner combine across windows

Walk $j = T{-}1$ down to $0$, applying $S \leftarrow S \cdot 2^c + W_j$.

$S \leftarrow$ $\mathcal{O}$ $\;=\; \mathcal{O}$  (identity, before any window)
$j = 1$:  $S \leftarrow S \cdot 2^3 + W_1$ → $\mathcal{O} \cdot 8 + W_1 \;=\; W_1$

$j = 0$:  $S \leftarrow S + W_0$ → $S = 8 W_1 + W_0$

For one MSM at $n = 2^{20}$, single threaded, on WASM:

Takeaways

  1. Pippenger reduces cost from $O(n\lambda)$ to $O(n\lambda/c + 2^c \lambda/c)$.
  2. Window decomposition + signed digits + suffix-sum = the algorithmic skeleton.
  3. The batched-affine + recirculation hot loop is the load-bearing optimisation. Without it Pippenger costs $\sim 11|M|$ per add. With it, $\sim 3.3|M|$.
  4. Parallelism: across inputs, not windows — each thread runs the full algorithm on its private bucket array.
  5. WASM-specific: $9 \times 29$ limb representation chosen to minimise inner mults under the no-overflow constraint.
  6. Net: a single MSM at $n = 2^{20}$ on 14-thread WASM-MT runs in $\approx 800$ ms.
