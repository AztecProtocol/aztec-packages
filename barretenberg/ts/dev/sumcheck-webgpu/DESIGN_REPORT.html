Offloading Chonk Sumcheck to WebGPU — Math & Design

How each stage computes, for the two engines that ship on sb/sumcheck-webgpu: the multi-pass engine (CPU in the round loop) and the single-submission engine (whole protocol in one command buffer, Fiat–Shamir on the GPU). MegaFlavor, non-ZK.

TL;DR. Both engines run the same \(O(n)\) work on the GPU — evaluate 14 relations per round into a flat 345-element accumulator, reduce over edges on the GPU, form the length-8 round univariate, then fold every column at the round challenge. They differ only in where the round loop lives. The multi-pass engine reads the univariate back to the CPU each round to draw the challenge (one blocking readback/round). The single-submission engine moves the batch tail and the Poseidon2 Fiat–Shamir transcript onto the GPU, encodes all \(d\) rounds into one command buffer, and reads back once. The math is identical; the data movement is not. On an M4 Pro, WebGPU crosses parity with threaded WASM at \(2^{15}\) and reaches 1.6–2.2× at \(2^{16}\!-\!2^{17}\); accumulate is \(85\%\) of GPU time, so the emulated 254-bit field multiply is the remaining lever — see §8–§9.

1  Scope

The sumcheck prover round has three stages: accumulate (\(O(n)\), evaluate every relation on every edge), batch (\(O(1)\), reduce to the length-8 round univariate), and fold (\(O(n)\), halve every column). Accumulate and fold are the GPU targets. The batch tail is \(O(1)\) — it runs on the CPU in the multi-pass engine and on the GPU in the single-submission engine. Both engines keep the polynomial columns GPU-resident in Montgomery byte form across all rounds.

Phase 0 ported the BN254 scalar-field Montgomery primitives to WGSL (add/sub/mul; no inversion — relations have no division). The 254-bit field element is held as 8×u32 limbs; each multiply is an emulated Montgomery product over 20×13-bit limbs (Karatsuba). Everything below builds on those.

2  The math both engines compute

Sumcheck proves, for a trace of \(n=2^d\) rows,

$$ \sum_{\vec x\in\{0,1\}^d}\;\operatorname{pow}_\beta(\vec x)\;F(\vec x)\;=\;\sigma, \qquad F(\vec x)=\sum_{g=0}^{62}\alpha^{g}\,g(\vec x), \qquad \operatorname{pow}_\beta(\vec x)=\prod_{k=0}^{d-1}\beta_k^{\,x_k}. $$

\(F\) is the \(\alpha\)-batched combination of the 63 subrelations (14 relations) of MegaFlavor; each \(g\) has algebraic degree \(\le 6\) (the per-round gate-separator factor lifts the batched round univariate to degree 7). \(\operatorname{pow}_\beta\) is the gate-separator (power) polynomial; its hypercube values are the beta-products \(B[m]=\prod_{j:\,\text{bit }j\text{ of }m}\beta_j\) (so \(B[0]=1\)).

2.1  The round univariate

In round \(i\) the prover has the polynomials partially evaluated at the first \(i\) challenges \(u_0,\dots,u_{i-1}\), leaving a hypercube of size \(m=2^{\,d-i}\). It sends the degree-\(\le 7\) univariate \(\tilde S^{\,i}(X)\), represented by its 8 evaluations at \(X=0,\dots,7\):

$$ \tilde S^{\,i}(X)\;=\;\underbrace{c_i\big((1-X)+X\beta_i\big)}_{\text{gate separator (this round)}} \cdot \!\!\sum_{g\in\mathrm{LI}}\alpha^{g}\,\widehat T^{\,i}_g(X) \;+\;\sum_{g\in\mathrm{LD}}\alpha^{g}\,\widehat T^{\,i}_g(X). $$

\(\mathrm{LI}/\mathrm{LD}\) are the linearly-independent / -dependent subrelations: only \(\mathrm{LI}\) ones carry the per-round gate-separator factor. The two per-round scalars are \(\beta_i\), this round's gate challenge, and the accumulated partial-evaluation product

$$ c_i=\prod_{k < i } \big((1-u_k)+u_k\beta_k\big),\qquad c_0=1. $$

2.2  Edge contributions \(T^{\,i}_g\)

The \(O(n)\) work is the edge sum. The current hypercube of size \(m\) is split into \(m/2\) edge pairs. For pair \(p\), each entity column \(P\) feeding \(g\) is lifted to the degree-1 line

$$ E_p[P](X)\;=\;(1-X)\,P[2p]\;+\;X\,P[2p+1], $$

and \(g\) is evaluated on those lines, giving a polynomial of degree \(\le\deg g\le 6\) in \(X\). Summed over pairs with the residual gate-separator weight,

$$ T^{\,i}_g(k)\;=\;\sum_{p=0}^{m/2-1}\; s^{\,(i)}_p\;\, g\!\big(E_p(k)\big), \qquad k=0,\dots,\deg g, \qquad \boxed{\,s^{\,(i)}_p=B\big[p\cdot 2^{\,i+1}\big]\,} $$

where \(s^{\,(i)}_p\) is the per-edge scaling (the gate-separator weight of pair \(p\), whose periodicity doubles every round), and \(L_g=\deg g+1\le 7\) is the subrelation's natural value-basis length. Because \(g(E_p(\cdot))\) has degree \(\le\deg g\), the \(L_g\) values \(T^{\,i}_g(0),\dots,T^{\,i}_g(L_g-1)\) determine a unique polynomial of that degree. \(\widehat T^{\,i}_g\) (used in §2.1) is that polynomial resampled on the length-8 domain \(\{0,\dots,7\}\) by Lagrange interpolation over the natural domain:

$$ \widehat T^{\,i}_g(X)\;=\;\sum_{j=0}^{L_g-1} T^{\,i}_g(j)\,\ell^{\,g}_j(X), \qquad \ell^{\,g}_j(X)=\!\!\prod_{\substack{0\le m < L_g\\ m\ne j}}\!\!\frac{X-m}{\,j-m\,}, \qquad X=0,\dots,7. $$

For \(X\in\{0,\dots,L_g-1\}\) this reproduces \(T^{\,i}_g(X)\) exactly; for \(X=L_g,\dots,7\) it supplies the higher evaluations. This is precisely C++ Univariate::extend_to — the barycentric extendTo in batch_tail.ts — and, being linear in \(T^{\,i}_g\), is what lets the single-submission engine fold the extension into the constant batch matrices \(M^{\mathrm{LI}},M^{\mathrm{LD}}\) of §4.C.

2.3  Fold (partial evaluation)

After the verifier challenge \(u_i\), every column is halved:

$$ P^{(i+1)}[k]\;=\;(1-u_i)\,P^{(i)}[2k]+u_i\,P^{(i)}[2k+1],\qquad k=0,\dots,\tfrac m2-1. $$
The telescoping identity (testing anchor). The verifier's per-round check is \(\tilde S^{\,i}(0)+\tilde S^{\,i}(1)=\tilde S^{\,i-1}(u_{i-1})\). For this non-ZK relation set it holds witness-independently — but only because the \(\mathrm{LD}\) subrelations carry no per-edge \(\beta\) weighting (weighting them breaks it). That pins a correctness requirement on the accumulate kernels and gives a black-box oracle over the whole chained pipeline.

3  Multi-pass engine — stage by stage

src: gpu_pipeline.ts · multiround.ts · batch_tail.ts · gate_separator.ts

The round loop lives on the CPU. Per round, two GPU submissions bracket one CPU tail: accumulate+reduce is submitted and its 345-element result is read back; the CPU forms the univariate and draws \(u_i\); a second submission folds. Columns never leave the GPU; only the 345-element accumulator and the length-8 univariate cross the boundary.

GPU · 1 thread/edge
A. Accumulate
Thread \(p\) of relation \(g\) gathers its edge straight from the resident column-major buffer (\(\texttt{col}[(j\!\gg\!1)\,m+2p+(j\,\&\,1)]\), per-column stride \(m\) = the current hypercube length), builds the degree-1 lines \(E_p\), evaluates the relation in a short-monomial form, and writes that edge's natural-degree evaluations, pre-scaled by \(s^{(i)}_p\): $$ \texttt{perEdge}[p\cdot\text{outLen}_g+k]\;=\;s^{(i)}_p\;g\!\big(E_p(k)\big),\quad k=0,\dots,\deg g. $$ No cross-edge reduction here — output is \(\text{outLen}_g\times\tfrac m2\).
GPU · two passes
B. Edge reduction (on-GPU, two-level)
Pass 1 partitions the \(\tfrac m2\) edges into \(\Gamma\le 64\) chunks; each workgroup sums its chunk: $$ \texttt{parts}[\gamma][k]=\!\!\sum_{p\in\text{chunk}_\gamma}\!\!\texttt{perEdge}[p][k]. $$ Pass 2 sums the \(\Gamma\) partials into one field element per column, written into the flat 345-element accumulator at this relation's offset: $$ A_g[k]=\sum_{\gamma}\texttt{parts}[\gamma][k]=\sum_{p}s^{(i)}_p\,g\!\big(E_p(k)\big)=T^{\,i}_g(k). $$ Only the 345-element \(A\) (\(\approx 11\) KB) is read back — not \(345\cdot\tfrac m2\).
CPU · \(O(1)\)
C. Batch tail \(\to\) round univariate (batchOverRelations)
With \(A\) decoded to canonical bigint: scale \(A_g\mapsto\alpha^{g}A_g\); extend each subrelation to length 8 by barycentric interpolation over \(\{0,\dots,L_g-1\}\); apply the pow factor \(\operatorname{extRandom}(e)=(1-e)+e\,\beta_i\) and \(c_i\) to \(\mathrm{LI}\), add \(\mathrm{LD}\) raw: $$ \tilde S^{\,i}[e]\;=\;c_i\,\operatorname{extRandom}(e)\!\!\sum_{g\in\mathrm{LI}}\widehat{\alpha^g A_g}(e) \;+\;\sum_{g\in\mathrm{LD}}\widehat{\alpha^g A_g}(e),\qquad e=0,\dots,7. $$ Cost \(\approx 63\) length-8 extensions — independent of \(n\).
CPU
D. Challenge
\(u_i\) is drawn from the round univariate (deterministic test value, or a supplied challenge chain). The gate separator advances: \(c_{i+1}=c_i\big((1-u_i)+u_i\beta_i\big)\), periodicity doubles, \(\beta_i\to\beta_{i+1}\). This read-back-to-draw is the one blocking CPU\(\leftrightarrow\)GPU sync per round.
GPU · 1 thread/output
E. Fold
A second submission halves every column at \(u_i\): \(P'[k]=(1-u_i)P[2k]+u_i P[2k+1]\), resident bytes \(\to\) resident bytes. Its output is the next round's columns (never decoded to canonical). No fence — the next round's accumulate read serializes against it via the queue.

4  Single-submission engine — stage by stage

src: single_submit.ts · batch_gpu.ts · poseidon2_gpu.ts

The entire \(d\)-round protocol is encoded into one command buffer and read back once. Stages A and B are identical to the multi-pass engine. The batch tail (C) and the challenge (D) move onto the GPU, so round \(i+1\)'s fold can consume \(u_i\) without ever returning to the host. Columns ping-pong between two resident sets (\(\texttt{colA}\leftrightarrow\texttt{colB}\)); WebGPU's intra-encoder hazard tracking serializes the dependent chain automatically.

GPU
A–B. Accumulate + edge reduction
Identical to §3.A–B: per-edge \(s^{(i)}_p\,g(E_p(k))\), two-level reduce \(\to\) the 345-element accumulator \(A\) in VRAM. It is not read back; it feeds the GPU batch kernel directly.
GPU · 8 lanes
C. Batch on the GPU (matrix reformulation)
The CPU tail is recast as two constant matrix–vector products. Because \(\alpha\) is fixed for the run and extend-to-8 is linear in \(A\), fold \(\alpha^g\) and the barycentric extend coefficients \(C^{(g)}_{e,j}\) (where \(\widehat{A_g}(e)=\sum_j C^{(g)}_{e,j}A_g[\mathrm{st}_g+j]\)) into matrices \(M^{\mathrm{LI}},M^{\mathrm{LD}}\in\mathbb F^{8\times 345}\), built once at setup: $$ M^{\mathrm{LI}}_{e,\,\mathrm{st}_g+j}=\begin{cases}\alpha^{g}\,C^{(g)}_{e,j}&g\in\mathrm{LI}\\[2pt]0&g\in\mathrm{LD}\end{cases} \qquad M^{\mathrm{LD}}=\text{(complement)}. $$ The kernel then computes, per eval point \(e=0,\dots,7\) (with \(a_e=(1-e),\,b_e=e\)): $$ \tilde S^{\,i}[e]=\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{idx}=0}^{344}M^{\mathrm{LI}}_{e,\mathrm{idx}}A[\mathrm{idx}]\Big) \big(a_e+b_e\beta_i\big)\,c_i\;+\;\sum_{\mathrm{idx}=0}^{344}M^{\mathrm{LD}}_{e,\mathrm{idx}}A[\mathrm{idx}]. $$ Only the per-round scalars \(\beta_i,c_i\) are read from VRAM; \(\beta_i\) is preloaded, \(c_i\) is the GPU-resident gate-separator product. Proven equal to the CPU batchOverRelations.
GPU · Poseidon2
D. Fiat–Shamir transcript + gate-separator advance
The round challenge is hashed on the GPU from that round's univariate, so the next fold can consume it in the same command buffer: $$ u_i=\operatorname{Poseidon2}\!\big(\,\text{run}_i\;\|\;\tilde S^{\,i}[0..7]\,\big),\qquad \text{run}_{i+1}=u_i,\quad \text{run}_0=\text{seed}, $$ with sponge IV \(=9\!\ll\!64\) (9 absorbed elements) and permutation \(t=4,\,R_F=8,\,R_P=56\) (\(\approx 3\) permutations/round). It then advances the resident gate separator $$ c_{i+1}=c_i\big((1-u_i)+u_i\beta_i\big) $$ in the same dispatch. This matches bb.js poseidon2Hash bit-for-bit, so the GPU-derived challenges are drop-in for the C++ transcript.
GPU · ping-pong
E. Fold (resident \(\to\) resident, swap)
Fold every column at \(u_i\) from \(\texttt{cur}\) into \(\texttt{other}\), then swap. The \(\texttt{batch}\to\texttt{transcript}\to\texttt{fold}\to\texttt{accumulate}\) dependency (incl. the \(c_i\)-read-before-\(c_{i+1}\)-write hazard) is enforced by encoder hazard tracking, not host fences.
One submit, one map. Each round copies its univariate and challenge into a staging buffer; after the loop a single submit + mapAsync returns all \(d\) univariates and \(d\) challenges. The host work per prove is then: upload columns, encode passes, one submit, one map, \(O(d)\) decode. (A second tiny submit currently fetches the final length-1 columns for the purported-value anchor — see §9.)

5  The threaded-WASM baseline (what we race against)

src: bench.ts → bb.js SumcheckBench · cpp/bbapi/bbapi_sumcheck.cpp · sumcheck.hpp · sumcheck_round.hpp

Production SumcheckProver<MegaFlavor>::prove() compiled to WASM, P pthreads on a SharedArrayBuffer heap (\(P=\) hardwareConcurrency). Same dense-random MegaFlavor, same non-ZK relations as the GPU engines. Each round \(i\) (size \(m=2^{d-i}\)) runs the four steps below; the math is §2, only the parallel axis differs.

P threads · over edges (chunks of 64)
A. Accumulate (compute_univariate)
Pairs \(\{0,\dots,\tfrac m2-1\}=\bigsqcup_{t} C_t\) work-stolen into thread-private accumulators, then merged: $$ A^{(t)}_g[k]=\!\!\sum_{p\in C_t}\! s_p\,g\!\big(E_p(k)\big),\qquad A_g[k]=\sum_{t < P}A^{(t)}_g[k]=T^{\,i}_g(k), \qquad s_p=\texttt{gate_separators}[2p]. $$
serial · \(O(1)\)
B. Batch (batch_over_relations)
Identical routine to §3.C: $$ \tilde S^{\,i}[e]=c_i\big((1-e)+e\beta_i\big)\!\!\sum_{g\in\mathrm{LI}}\!\widehat{\alpha^g A_g}(e)\;+\!\!\sum_{g\in\mathrm{LD}}\!\widehat{\alpha^g A_g}(e),\qquad e=0,\dots,7. $$
serial
C. Challenge (transcript)
$$ u_i=\operatorname{Poseidon2\text{-}Transcript}\big(\tilde S^{\,i}[0..7]\big). $$
P threads · over the \(N\!\approx\!185\) columns
D. Fold (partially_evaluate)
$$ P^{(i+1)}_j[k]=P^{(i)}_j[2k]+u_i\big(P^{(i)}_j[2k+1]-P^{(i)}_j[2k]\big),\qquad j=0,\dots,N-1,\ \ k=0,\dots,\tfrac m2-1. $$
Step Parallel axis (WASM) vs GPU axis field mul
A accumulate P threads · edge chunks 1 lane / edge (\(m/2\)) native i64 limbs
(vs emulated \(8\times\)u32)
D fold P threads · columns (\(\le 185\)) 1 lane / output

WASM's scalar multiply is faster but parallelism is capped at \(P\!\sim\!10\!-\!12\); the GPU runs thousands of edge-lanes, so it overtakes from \(2^{16}\) (§8–§9). Both draw \(u_i\) serially between rounds.

Headline result. WebGPU crosses parity with threaded WASM at \(2^{15}\) and wins 1.6–2.2× at \(2^{16}\!-\!2^{17}\) — the GPU's edge-parallelism beats WASM at scale despite the emulated 254-bit multiply. 7.3  Hybrid size sweep — fixed 9-round WASM tail (2¹⁰–2¹⁸)

Plot F swept the split point \(k\) at a fixed \(2^{18}\). This sweep fixes the strategy — fold the first \(d-9\) rounds on WebGPU, hand the last 9 (a \(\le 2^9\) hypercube) to threaded WASM — and sweeps the size. It is the configuration a real prover would ship: the heavy early rounds, where edge-parallelism pays, run on the GPU; the cheap tail, where the GPU is launch-bound, stays on WASM.

Headline. crosses parity at \(2^{14}\), climbs to 3.22× at \(2^{18}\) — still rising — and the gap widens with size, because the GPU front grows ~1.8×/octave against full-WASM's ~2.05×. At \(2^{18}\) the 9-round GPU front (656.7 ms) carries essentially the whole wall; the WASM tail and handoff are a rounding error. This is the engine with the GPU-resident gate-separator scaling of §3 — the host-bigint cliff that previously inflated the deep-round wall at \(2^{18}\) is gone.

Directions this suggests. (1) The crossover (\(2^{14}\)) is set by fixed cost, not throughput — a flat ~13 ms WASM tail (always the last 9 rounds) plus GPU launch latency. To pull it left, cut the GPU front's fixed cost rather than the tail (small-\(n\) GPU rounds are launch-bound, not compute-bound — §9), or shrink the tail toward \(\le 2^6\) and re-measure. (2) Handoff is free (1–3 ms, no trend) — the GPU→WASM column transfer is not a bottleneck, so the split length is a tunable knob, not a fixed cost. (3) The win is slope, not offset — GPU front ~1.8×/octave vs WASM ~2.05×, so \(2^{19}\!-\!2^{20}\) should pass 4×; keeping the GPU-front slope sub-WASM (the §3 scaling fix, then the accumulate field-multiply of §9) is the lever that compounds. (4) The tail is a floor worth attacking only at small \(n\) — ~2% of wall at \(2^{18}\) but ~34% at \(2^{10}\).

7.4  Engine × threshold — single-submission vs multi-pass front (2¹⁰–2¹⁸)

§7.3 fixed the engine (multi-pass) and the tail (9 rounds). Here both open up: the same threshold split — last \(T\) rounds on WASM, first \(d-T\) on the GPU — is run on both engines, the multi-pass front (one readback per round, CPU Fiat–Shamir) and the single-submission front (the whole \(d-T\)-round front in one command buffer, on-GPU Poseidon2 Fiat–Shamir with the lane-parallel transcript of §9), across \(T\in\{8,9,10,11\}\). Speedup is full-WASM ÷ hybrid (tail timed as SumcheckBench(T); the one-time column upload excluded from both, as in §7.3). This isolates the one thing that differs — how the round challenge is drawn — against size.

Headline. the engines have converged — within ~10% across the range, trading the lead at the top — with the parallel transcript and GPU-resident scaling, single-submission is no longer the clear loser the original report measured (when its serial @workgroup_size(1) transcript cost 27% of GPU time). Multi-pass keeps a slight edge through \(2^{17}\) (smaller per-round GPU overhead), but at \(2^{18}\) the per-round readback sync it pays starts to bite and single-submission edges ahead — the single best result in the whole sweep is single-submission, \(T=9\), \(2^{18}\): 3.05× (vs multi-pass's 2.95× at the same split). The isolated GPU-front timing is cleaner than the WASM-relative ratios here: at \(2^{18}\) the single-submission front is ~700 ms for 9 rounds vs the multi-pass front's ~1050 ms — ~1.5× — the readback sync the single command buffer removes.

9  Critical analysis — where the time goes

The M4 data (§8) reframes the priorities. WebGPU is overhead-bound below \(2^{15}\) (it loses to WASM on launch latency), crosses parity at \(2^{15}\), and wins by edge-parallelism at \(2^{16}\!-\!2^{17}\). Accumulate is \(85.6\%\) of round-0 GPU time, so the GPU is doing real \(O(n)\) field work — the remaining levers are the field multiply and two engine-specific scaling cliffs.

The two engines fail differently, and the data shows exactly how. The single-submission engine spends 27.3% of its GPU time in the serial Poseidon2 transcript (5.46 ms/round, @workgroup_size(1)) — that cost exceeds the per-round readbacks it was built to remove, which is why multi-pass is faster at every size \(\le 2^{17}\) (e.g. \(2^{17}\): 487 ms vs 592 ms). The multi-pass engine inverts at \(2^{18}\): its sweep wall jumps to 1993 ms (vs single-submit's 1353), a host-side scaling cliff — the \(O(n\log n)\) gate-separator bigint precompute and per-round fold allocation. (That row is noisy: the hybrid harness timed the same engine/size at 1027 ms, so treat \(2^{18}\) multi-pass as variance-prone, not a stable 1993 ms.) Fixing either unblocks the other's weak size.

What is already efficient

  • On-GPU two-level edge reduction — one ~11 KB readback/round (multi-pass) or none (single-submit), not \(345\cdot\tfrac n2\).
  • Per-edge output is the relation's natural degree; the extend-to-8 happens once on the 345-vector (or as a constant matmul on the GPU), not per edge.
  • Montgomery residency + ping-pong: columns never re-encode or leave byte form across rounds.
  • Batch as a precomputed \(8\times345\) matmul folds \(\alpha^g\) and the barycentric extension into setup-time constants.

10  Gaps before a real chonk integration

  • Shared 62-column flavor. The dev harness gives each relation independent random columns; the real prover has ~62 entity columns shared across relations with a fixed column→input mapping.
  • Shift aliasing. Shifted entities (\(w_{\text{shift}}\) = row\(+1\)) are modelled as separate columns; the real flavor reads them as an offset of the base, halving column storage and fold work.
  • Transcript wiring. The GPU Poseidon2 transcript already matches bb.js bit-for-bit, so the single-submission challenges are drop-in — provided the rest of the proof transcript (commitments, etc.) is hashed consistently and the witness arrives already in Montgomery bytes.
  • Output handoff. The final length-1 columns are exactly the claimed multilinear evaluations the PCS/opening consumes (the §9 item-4 readback already produces them).
  • ZK / Libra / row-disabling are out of scope (non-ZK MegaFlavor only). On this architecture ZK is additive: a masking term folded into the accumulator pre-batch, one wider univariate (8→9), and the masking randomness committed into the GPU transcript — the single-command-buffer shape survives.
